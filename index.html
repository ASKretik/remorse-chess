Remorse Chess

Chess, but every capture haunts the capturer.

  • All normal chess rules apply.
  • After every capture the killer rolls a die to see how it takes it:
    • 1 · Devastated — can't move or give check. Absolve it within 3 of your turns or it ends itself.
    • 2–3 · Remorseful — can't capture or give check until absolved.
    • 4–5 · Unfazed — carries on as normal.
    • 6 · Psychopath — permanent, never rolls again. Each kill earns it one extra move (no chaining). After 3 kills it gets bloodlust: it only moves to capture, and will kill its own side.
  • Absolution: a bishop that moves to a square orthogonally next to (up, down, left, right) a remorseful or devastated piece absolves it instantly. The piece moving next to a bishop doesn't count.
  • Instead of moving, you can spend your turn having one of your bishops absolve every afflicted piece next to it.
  • An afflicted bishop can't absolve anyone, but you can spend a turn to let it absolve itself.

Rules

Every capture makes the killer roll a die: 1 devastated (can't move; absolve within 3 turns or it ends itself), 2–3 remorseful (can't capture), 4–5 unfazed, 6 psychopath (an extra move after each kill; after 3 kills bloodlust: it only moves to capture, friend or foe). Devastated and remorseful pieces give no check.

A healthy bishop (either colour) moving orthogonally next to an afflicted piece absolves it. Or spend your turn letting one of your bishops absolve everything next to it (select it, press Absolve). An afflicted bishop can only absolve itself. Everything else is standard chess.
